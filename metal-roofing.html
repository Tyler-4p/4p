A Roof You Won't Think About Again

Metal roofing costs more up front than asphalt shingles, and it's worth being straight about that. What you get in return is a roof that holds up to hail, wind, and Texas heat far longer than a standard shingle roof, sheds water and debris better, and reflects heat instead of absorbing it — which can mean a real difference in cooling costs during the summer.

We install standing-seam and metal panel systems the same way we approach every roof: proper decking, correct underlayment, and installation to manufacturer spec — not just screwed down and called finished.

What Makes Metal Better Than Shingle

Asphalt shingles are a good, affordable roof. Metal is a different kind of roof, and the differences show up over the long run:

  • Longevity. A properly installed metal roof is built to go decades before it needs to be replaced, well past what a typical shingle roof delivers. That's why metal often costs less over the life of the home, even though it costs more on day one.
  • Storm resistance. Metal panels stand up to hail and high wind far better than standard asphalt shingles. Large hail can still leave cosmetic dents on some metal panels, so panel gauge and finish matter, and we'll walk you through the options.
  • Heat. Metal reflects heat instead of absorbing it, which can help lower cooling costs through the Texas summer.
  • Water and debris. Smooth metal panels shed water and debris quickly instead of holding onto it.
  • Fire resistance. Metal doesn't burn, and metal roof systems typically carry the highest fire rating.
  • Low maintenance. No shingles to lose granules, curl, or crack, so there's less to repair over the years.

Standing Seam vs. R-Panel

Not all metal roofs are the same. The two systems we work with are standing seam and R-panel, and the biggest difference between them is the fasteners.

Fasteners are the screws that hold the panels down. On one system they're hidden. On the other they're out in the open. That one difference affects how the roof looks, how it holds up over the years, and what it costs.

STANDING SEAMHidden Fasteners

Standing-seam panels have raised vertical seams that lock together. The fasteners are concealed under the panels with hidden clips, so there are no screw heads on the surface of the roof.

  • A clean, smooth look with no visible screws
  • Fewer exposed openings where water could find a way in
  • Panels can expand and contract with the heat on their clips, instead of being fastened right through the panel
  • Generally the longer-lasting system, and the one most homeowners choose for their house
  • Costs more than R-panel, because the panels and installation are more involved

R-PANELExposed Fasteners

R-panel has a ribbed profile and is screwed down through the face of the panel. The fasteners are exposed, with a rubber washer under each screw head to seal it.

  • A more affordable way to get a metal roof
  • Quicker to install, and a good fit for many homes, outbuildings, and shops
  • A ribbed, more industrial look, with screw heads visible across the roof
  • The fasteners and washers should be checked from time to time, since the washers can wear over the years and may need to be tightened or replaced

Which One Is Right for You?

It comes down to your budget, how you want the roof to look, and how long you plan to be in the home. If you want the cleanest look and the longest life, standing seam is usually the better fit. If you want the benefits of metal at a lower price, R-panel can be a good choice.

We'll look at your home, explain the tradeoffs in plain terms, and give you our honest recommendation, with no pressure to choose either one.
