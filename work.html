Replacement, Repair, or Inspection? How to Decide.

Not every roof problem calls for a new roof, and not every roof needs work right now. Here's a plain-language look at when each makes sense. If you're ever unsure, a free inspection will tell you where your roof stands.

When to Get a Roof Replacement

A replacement makes sense when the problems are widespread, or when the roof has simply reached the end of its useful life. Common signs include:

  • The roof is old. Many asphalt shingle roofs reach the end of their life somewhere around 20 to 30 years, depending on the shingle and conditions.
  • Storm damage covers large areas of the roof, such as hail or wind damage across several slopes.
  • Shingles are curling, cracking, or losing granules across much of the roof.
  • You're dealing with repeated leaks, or repairs keep adding up without solving the problem.
  • The decking underneath is soft, sagging, or damaged.

When a Roof Repair Is Enough

A repair is the right call when the damage is limited and the rest of the roof is in good shape. Typical examples include:

  • A few missing, lifted, or damaged shingles after a storm.
  • A leak that traces back to one spot, such as flashing, a vent, or a pipe boot.
  • Damage in one small area of an otherwise healthy, newer roof.

Fixing small problems promptly usually costs far less than waiting, since water that gets in can damage the decking, insulation, and ceilings.

When to Schedule a Roof Inspection

You don't have to wait for a leak. An inspection is a good idea:

  • After a hailstorm or high winds, even if you don't see obvious damage from the ground.
  • Once a year or so, especially as your roof gets older.
  • When you notice warning signs, such as ceiling stains, shingle pieces in the yard, granules in the gutters, or daylight in the attic.
  • Before you buy or sell a home.
  • Before you decide between a repair and a replacement, so you know what the roof actually needs.
