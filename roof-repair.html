Not Every Problem Needs a New Roof

A leak, a missing shingle, or storm damage isolated to one section of the roof usually doesn't call for a full tear-off. It calls for someone who actually traces the leak back to its source instead of just patching the spot where the water showed up inside the house — because those are rarely the same spot.

We inspect the full roof, not just the reported problem area, so we can tell you honestly whether a repair is the right call or whether the damage is more widespread than it looks from the ground. If it's a repair, we fix it right. If it's not, we'll tell you that too.

When a Repair Makes Sense

A repair is usually the right call when the damage is limited and the rest of the roof is in good shape. Signs you might need one:

  • A ceiling stain or leak that just appeared
  • A handful of shingles missing after a storm
  • Flashing pulled loose around a chimney or vent
  • Damage limited to one section of the roof
  • A roof under 10 years old with isolated damage
  • You just want a second opinion before a bigger job

Fixing small problems promptly usually costs far less than waiting, since water that gets in can damage the decking, insulation, and ceilings.
